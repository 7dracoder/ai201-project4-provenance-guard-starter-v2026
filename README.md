# Provenance Guard

## What This Does

1. POST /submit receives JSON containing text and creator_id.
2. The route checks that both are nonempty strings, text is at most 10,000 characters, and (after the Unit 8 improvement) text contains at least three alphabetic word runs of two or more letters.
3. detector.model_signal reads the text with a local GPT-2 language model and measures predictability.
4. stylometry.style_signal measures sentence length spread, word variety, and punctuation density.
5. scoring.combine_signals combines the two scores into a number from 0 to 1.
6. scoring.score_to_label selects a full sentence label, and audit.log_decision records the decision with a unique content_id.
7. The response returns content_id, guess, confidence, label, model_score, style_score, and status. A writer can send content_id and reasoning to POST /appeal; the service records under_review and confirms without rescoring.

A human writer can be wrongly flagged. For example, a score of 0.90 receives the AI label below, but the writer can appeal using the returned content_id. The original decision stays in the log beside the appeal.

## Signals and Scoring

Planned rule, written before implementation: scoring.py::combine_signals uses 0.7 * model_score + 0.3 * style_score. Weights live in config.py. Higher means more likely AI. A model score of 0.9 and style score of 0.1 combine to 0.66, which remains unsure.

The model signal in detector.py::model_signal measures how predictable words are in context to GPT-2. Generated text can be predictable, but ordinary human writing can be too. It cannot prove authorship and only reads the first 400 tokens.

The style signal in stylometry.py::style_signal measures shape without meaning. Regular sentence lengths, repeated vocabulary, and little punctuation increase its AI score. It cannot understand content; typos, quotations, text length, and deliberate sentence changes can fool it.

Planned style rule: average 1 - min(sentence-length coefficient of variation, 1), 1 - unique-word ratio, and 1 - min(punctuation density / 0.1, 1). Spread is sample standard deviation divided by mean sentence word count. Punctuation density is ASCII punctuation characters divided by total characters. Fewer than two sentences or ten words gives a neutral style score of 0.5.

## Label Variants

| Guess | Combined score | Exact label |
|---|---|---|
| human | 0 <= score <= 0.20 | We think this was written by a person, but this estimate is not proof of authorship. |
| unsure | 0.20 < score < 0.80 | We are unsure who wrote this text. This estimate should not be treated as an accusation. You can request a review. |
| ai | 0.80 <= score <= 1 | We think this may have been generated by AI, but we could be wrong. You can request a review if you wrote it yourself. |

The wide unsure range is intended to reduce wrong accusations. The confidence field is the combined AI score, rather than the probability that a guess is correct.

## Rate Limiting

The limits are 10 submissions per minute and 200 per day, enabled by default on POST /submit. Ten per minute allows a writer to submit a few revisions while stopping a rapid probe after ten requests; 200 per day permits a busy writing session but caps sustained probing. The starter counts a supplied creator_id and falls back to the caller address when it is missing or unusable; this existing behavior is unchanged. A caller can evade it by changing creator_id, and people sharing an address can affect one another when no ID is supplied. Counts are in memory and reset when the service restarts.

Every decision already records timestamp, content_id, creator_id, guess, model_score, style_score, combined_score, label, and status. This unit also records route rejections, as well as the starter's rate-limit rejections. The original append-only decision remains beside any appeal; the newest event gives the current status.

### Verdict reasoning and diagnoses

1. **MET on the substitute corpus:** all three trials flagged 0 of 10 human samples as AI, within the original maximum of 1. This does not establish performance on my personal writing or on other genres.
2. **MISSED:** every trial had human mean 0.55863, AI mean 0.60610, and difference 0.04747, below 0.20. The stages are signal one, signal two, and the combining rule: human/AI model means were 0.58928/0.62374 and style means were 0.48710/0.56496. Predictability and surface shape overlap across these groups. The unchanged rule calculates 0.7 * 0.03446 + 0.3 * 0.07786 = 0.04747; it cannot create separation absent from its inputs. This is a limitation of the signals on this corpus, rather than a label-mapping gap.
3. **MET:** each trial submitted the same 12 inputs, reached ai/human/unsure, and kept all scores in [0, 1]. The contrived fixtures prove reachability, not correct authorship.
4. **MET:** every trial recorded 5 of 5 appeals as under_review with exact reasoning and the original decision retained. The evidence checks both HTTP responses and audit entries.
5. **MET:** all six specified invalid inputs returned JSON 400 in every trial, with zero additional decisions. The extra rejection events are expected and are not decisions.

## The Improvement

Planned after the baseline: add one input-validation rule in app.py::submit requiring at least three alphabetic word runs of two or more letters before calling either signal. This follows MF03-MF06: the route currently admits material that lacks enough words to support a prose authorship estimate. The existing length/type/creator checks, both signals, weights, thresholds, labels, and all five criteria will stay the same. The rule uses Unicode letters so that Arabic is not rejected merely for its script.

This change may reject legitimate two-word poems or captions. It is meant to stop misleading decisions on obvious non-prose input; it is not expected to repair score separation or evasion. The after run will measure the trade rather than assume it helped.

Nine evasion attacks remain BROKE under the rule that avoiding an AI label counts as evasion. They receive unsure, not a human certification. The pipeline diagnoses above still apply after the fix; valid inputs are processed exactly as before.

I stopped after the one documented validation improvement so that its effect could be measured without mixing in changes to signals, weights, or labels. The repeats are stable, but a ten-paragraph corpus from one book is narrow, the AI samples come from one generator, and the deliberately shaped label fixtures are not accuracy evidence. The assignment's own-writing test still needs ten samples I actually wrote; this run does not substitute published text and claim it is mine.

The new rule can reject legitimate short poems, captions, or writing in scripts without space-separated words, while still accepting arbitrary junk containing three qualifying words. The caller identifier is also self-reported, so rotating it bypasses the cap, and an in-memory restart resets counts. These limitations are documented rather than patched alongside the single improvement.

For a real writing site, I would prefer more unsure labels and a reliable human review path over accusing a writer on weak evidence. The site and its reviewers would bear the extra review work and some AI writing would pass through, while writers would face fewer unsupported accusations.
